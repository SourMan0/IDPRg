1️⃣ Mean RMSE by Regression Type and PCA Components

What it shows: Each group of bars represents the mean RMSE for a particular number of PCA components (10, 20, 50, 100, 190) across regression models (GPR, Kernel Ridge, Lasso, Linear, Ridge).

Interpretation:

Lower bars = better models (since RMSE measures prediction error).

Most regressors perform similarly for low PCA dimensions (10–50).

However, Linear Regression performs poorly at PCA = 100 and PCA = 190 — its RMSE spikes dramatically, suggesting instability or overfitting in those configurations.

GPR and Kernel Ridge consistently maintain low RMSEs, indicating they’re robust across PCA settings.

What it shows: Each point is one model configuration, with:

X-axis: RMSE (lower is better)

Y-axis: R² (higher is better)

Color: Different random seeds

Interpretation:

The top-left corner (high R², low RMSE) indicates the best models.

Most points cluster near R² = 0, meaning many models don’t explain much variance in the data.

Some points have strongly negative R², showing they perform worse than just predicting the mean.

There’s no major visible bias by seed — the color mix is fairly uniform — suggesting that model performance is consistent across seeds.

Only a small subset of configurations achieve both low RMSE and high R² — those would be your top-performing runs.

3️⃣ RMSE Distribution per Seed (Box Plot)

What it shows: The spread of RMSE scores for each seed.

Interpretation:

The boxes are quite similar, meaning each seed yields roughly the same error distribution — this indicates stable training across random initializations.

There are outliers (circles above the whiskers) — those are bad runs with unusually high RMSE.

Median RMSEs across seeds are all near 0.3–0.4, reinforcing that no single seed performed drastically better or worse overall.

🧩 Overall Takeaway

The best-performing regressors are likely GPR and Kernel Ridge.

Linear Regression performs inconsistently and often poorly at higher PCA dimensions.

Seed variation has minimal effect, suggesting your models are not overly sensitive to initialization.

Focus future tuning on PCA ≤ 50 and GPR/Kernel Ridge models — that’s where performance is most reliable.
